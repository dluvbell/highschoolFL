How a mission works 4 random cards, one twist, one rule that's yours.

Each mission draws 4 cards at random from that topic's question pool — replay a mission and you will get different questions, in a different order, until you have seen the whole pool. You get 5 seconds per card — set your own time in the Time panel above, and change it anytime, even mid-mission. Your money counter moves the instant you choose. Watch for the twist card: life does not follow your plan. Each mission also shows one random quote — always from that topic, never a random topic — with its source. At the end, you write one rule that is yours.

Sources are always labelled: School (Ontario financial literacy), Seungho Kim (The Properties of Money), or Housel (The Psychology of Money). Knowing who said it keeps any one voice from becoming “the truth.” Seungho Kim calls earning, saving, keeping and spending four different abilities — like four legs of a table; growing your money is the fifth we practise here.

The ideas behind the game

Seungho Kim — the 5 properties of money
  • Money is a person. Treat it with respect and it stays; treat it carelessly and it leaves.
  • The power of regular income. Money that arrives steadily is worth more than the same amount arriving randomly.
  • Every kind of money has its own character. Earned, borrowed, gifted and won money all behave differently in your hands.
  • Money has gravity. Money gathers where money already is — small amounts pull in more.
  • How you treat other people's money is how you treat your own.
Seungho Kim — the abilities (the table's legs)
  • Earn — the visible one. Skills, work, and raising the value of your time. Earning well does not make you wealthy by itself.
  • Save — balance and detail: budgets, separate accounts, knowing rates, interest and tax. Good earners are not automatically good savers.
  • Guard — keeping what you built. The hardest leg: emergencies, scams, lifestyle creep, debt.
  • Spend — almost a political skill. Be frugal without being stingy; pay what you owe, on the day you promised.
  • Grow (added for this game) — investing and compounding. Money that never grows quietly melts to inflation.
Morgan Housel — The Psychology of Money, all 20 chapters
    Ontario school financial literacy — the skills layer
    • Pay stubs: gross vs. net, CPP, EI, income tax, marginal vs. effective rates
    • Budgeting: needs vs. wants, 50/30/20, fixed vs. variable costs, subscriptions and BNPL
    • Saving: a 3–6 month emergency fund first, CDIC protection, pay-yourself-first
    • Interest: simple vs. compound, the Rule of 72, inflation
    • Credit: scores, minimum-payment traps, ~20% card interest, good debt vs. bad debt, OSAP
    • Canadian accounts: TFSA (18+), FHSA, RRSP, RESP, GICs
    • Investing basics: stocks, bonds, ETFs, diversification, fees, capital gains
    • Staying safe: CRA impersonation scams, phishing, identity theft, the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre
    • Grade 10 note: financial literacy is assessed in Career Studies (GLC2O) as part of the graduation requirement.
    For teachers — sharing this game on GitHub Pages

    This game is a single static index.html file — no server, no build step, no accounts. To share it with a class:

    • Create a GitHub repository and add this file as index.html.
    • In the repo, open Settings → Pages, choose “Deploy from a branch”, pick your main branch and the root folder, and save.
    • GitHub gives you a public link (like yourname.github.io/repo). Send that link to students — it just works.
    • Each student's progress saves only in their own browser (localStorage) — no names, no emails, no Google accounts, nothing uploaded anywhere. To move to another device, students tap Copy my save code (the game state rides inside the code) or scan their QR code; nobody should ever have to retype the long code. Scan-and-continue is built in: this file's published address — https://dluvbell.github.io/highschoolFL/ — is hardcoded as GAME_URL in the script and locked, so every QR holds that address + the save, and scanning opens the game already loaded on any device. It cannot be changed from the browser; to move the game, edit GAME_URL in index.html and re-upload.
    • Students may also replace their random short code with their own pick — even 1234, a class number, or a 4-word code. A short code loads a save in that browser only, so if you assign codes, keep them unique per student: two students with the same code on one shared device share one save.
